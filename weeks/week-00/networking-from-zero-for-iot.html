Networking From Zero for IoT

Compare enterprise IP networking with constrained PAN/LPWAN topologies.

40 min read Level: MS foundation → advanced Status: Full lesson

1. Introduction

If your networking intuition comes from enterprise IT, it will mislead you here. Enterprise networking optimizes for throughput and manageability with effectively unlimited power. IoT networking optimizes for years of battery life and kilometers of range on milliwatts — and every security property downstream of that trade-off looks different as a result.

2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Compare enterprise IP networking with constrained PAN/LPWAN topologies.
  • Explain why IoT networking optimizes for power and range over throughput.
  • Place a device correctly in a star, mesh, or hub-and-spoke topology.

3. Prerequisites

No prior lessons required — this is the course's starting point.

4. Core Concepts

Three topology families cover most IoT deployments:

  • Star — every device talks directly to a central hub or gateway (common in Zigbee coordinator setups, most Wi-Fi based devices).
  • Mesh — devices relay traffic for each other, extending range at the cost of more complex routing and a larger attack surface for routing-layer attacks (Zigbee mesh, Thread).
  • Hub-and-spoke via LPWAN — long-range, low-power wide-area networks (LoRaWAN, NB-IoT) where devices talk long distances directly to a base station, trading bandwidth for range and battery life.

Underneath topology, almost every LPWAN and many mesh protocols use duty cycling: a device budgets how many seconds per hour it's allowed to transmit, both for battery life and, in unlicensed spectrum, for regulatory compliance. This single constraint explains a huge amount of IoT protocol design — including why security handshakes are often deliberately minimized.

7. Mathematical / Formal Foundations

Duty cycle budgets are simple but consequential: a device limited to a 1% duty cycle can transmit for only 36 seconds per hour. That budget has to cover sensor data and any security overhead — retransmissions, key renegotiation, or extra authentication rounds all compete for the same 36 seconds.

8. Practical Examples

Worked example: a LoRaWAN soil-moisture sensor wakes once per hour, sends an 11-byte payload, and sleeps. If a security control added a full TLS-style handshake on every wake cycle, it could single-handedly consume the device's entire duty-cycle and power budget — which is exactly why LoRaWAN uses a lightweight join procedure instead (covered in Week 7).

10. Security Perspective

Power and airtime budgets are why so many IoT protocols choose "authenticate once, trust the session for a long time" designs — which is efficient, but means a compromised session key can remain valid far longer than a typical web session token would.

11. Industry Perspective

Network operators deploying LPWAN at city scale plan spectrum and duty-cycle budgets the way cloud teams plan compute capacity — it's a first-class constraint in the architecture, not an afterthought, and security features must be costed against it explicitly.

12. Research Perspective

A recurring research theme is designing authentication and key-rotation schemes that fit inside these tiny power and airtime budgets without falling back to "authenticate once, forever" — this is still an active area, not a solved problem.

13. Case Study

Several LPWAN sensor-network compromises have traced back to long-lived session keys that were never rotated because rotation was considered "too expensive" for the duty-cycle budget — illustrating the real tension between power efficiency and cryptographic hygiene that this lesson introduces.

14. Common Misconceptions

  • "IoT networking is just Wi-Fi with less range." — Most IoT protocols make fundamentally different power/range/bandwidth trade-offs than Wi-Fi.
  • "Mesh networks are always more secure because there's no single point of failure." — Mesh adds routing-layer attack surface (route poisoning, relay abuse) that star topologies don't have.
  • "Duty cycle limits are just a regulatory nuisance." — They actively shape which security designs are even feasible on a device's power budget.

16. Glossary

LPWAN
Low-Power Wide-Area Network — long-range, low-bandwidth radio designed for battery-powered sensors (e.g., LoRaWAN, NB-IoT).
Duty cycle
The fraction of time a device is permitted or able to transmit, often regulatory or power-driven.
Gateway
A device that bridges a constrained IoT network to IP-based networks, often performing protocol translation.

17. Self-Assessment

1. Why might an IoT protocol avoid a full handshake on every message?

Duty-cycle and battery budgets are so tight on many IoT devices that repeating an expensive handshake every message could consume most of the available airtime.

2. What new attack surface does a mesh topology introduce compared to a star topology?

Because mesh devices relay traffic for each other, a compromised or malicious node can manipulate routing in ways a star topology's central hub design avoids.

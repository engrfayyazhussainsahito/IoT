Fleet-Scale IoT Security Assessment & Risk Register

Produce a fleet-scale risk register from an assessment.

45 min read Level: MS foundation → advanced Status: Full lesson

1. Introduction

This is the capstone's first working deliverable: taking everything from the Week 8 midterm's single-deployment assessment method and scaling it to a full fictional fleet, producing the portfolio-level risk register Week 14 taught you to build.

2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Produce a fleet-scale risk register from an assessment.
  • Prioritize findings using safety-aware risk scoring.

3. Prerequisites

Week 0 foundations, plus Weeks 0–15 of this course.

4. Core Concepts

The fleet assessment methodology combines, in sequence: Week 1's trust-boundary architecture mapping, Week 13's reconnaissance-and-validation approach (applied here to the capstone's provided artifacts, not live testing), and Week 14's risk-tiered register structure — scaled from the midterm's single deployment to a fictional multi-site, multi-product-line fleet.

The risk register structure should let a reader compare findings across very different asset types (a consumer sensor line versus an industrial actuator line) on a common, safety-aware scale (Week 1's Likelihood × [Digital Impact + Safety Impact] formula), exactly as Week 14's portfolio risk management lesson described.

Safety-aware prioritization means the final deliverable's ranking should visibly reflect physical consequence, not just technical severity — a lower-CVSS finding on a safety-relevant actuator should plausibly outrank a higher-CVSS finding on a pure-telemetry sensor, if the underlying scoring is done correctly.

7. Mathematical / Formal Foundations

Reuse Week 1's formula exactly: Risk = Likelihood (1-5) × [Digital Impact (1-5) + Safety Impact (0-5)], applied consistently across every finding regardless of which product line it came from — consistency across asset types is what makes a portfolio-level register genuinely comparable, per Week 14.

8. Practical Examples

Deliverable format: a risk register (spreadsheet or structured document) with one row per finding, columns for asset/product line, finding description, likelihood, digital impact, safety impact, computed risk score, and recommended remediation — sorted by risk score, with a short narrative explaining any cases where a lower-score finding was still flagged for expedited attention due to qualitative factors the formula doesn't fully capture.

10. Security Perspective

This deliverable is graded on the same criteria as the Week 8 midterm, scaled up: correct trust-boundary identification, consistent safety-aware scoring application, and defensible prioritization — not on raw finding count or technical sophistication of any single finding.

11. Industry Perspective

This mirrors exactly how a real security consultancy or internal security team would structure a portfolio-wide risk assessment deliverable for executive and engineering stakeholders simultaneously, per Week 14's governance-reporting material.

12. Research Perspective

No new research content here — the goal is disciplined application of prior weeks' frameworks at scale, the same integration principle the Week 8 midterm established for a single deployment.

13. Case Study

Past cohorts' most common capstone deduction at this stage: inconsistent scoring methodology applied across different product lines (using CVSS for one, an ad hoc scale for another), breaking the portfolio-level comparability that's the entire point of a unified risk register.

14. Common Misconceptions

  • "Different product lines can reasonably use different risk-scoring scales." — Consistent scoring is essential for a portfolio register to support genuine cross-product prioritization.
  • "More findings documented automatically means a stronger deliverable." — Consistent, defensible scoring and prioritization matter more than raw finding count.
  • "This lesson requires new technical skills beyond what the course has already covered." — It's explicitly an integration exercise combining Weeks 1, 13, and 14's existing methods at larger scale.

16. Glossary

Fleet-scale assessment
A security assessment methodology applied across an entire portfolio of products or deployments, rather than a single asset.

17. Self-Assessment

1. Why is scoring consistency across different product lines essential for a fleet-scale risk register?

A portfolio risk register's value comes from enabling comparison and prioritization across different assets; inconsistent scoring methodology across product lines undermines that core purpose.

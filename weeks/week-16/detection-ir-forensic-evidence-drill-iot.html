Detection/IR/Forensic Evidence Drill for an IoT Incident

Run a full detection-to-forensics drill on a simulated IoT incident.

45 min read Level: MS foundation → advanced Status: Full lesson

1. Introduction

The capstone's second deliverable moves from static assessment to dynamic response: a full drill simulating detection through forensic evidence packaging, integrating Week 11's detection material, Week 12's IR and forensics material, and Week 9's fleet-scale telemetry concepts into one exercise.

2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson you will be able to:

  • Run a full detection-to-forensics drill on a simulated IoT incident.
  • Produce evidence and a containment/recovery narrative.

3. Prerequisites

Week 0 foundations, plus Weeks 0–15 of this course.

4. Core Concepts

The drill structure follows the full incident lifecycle end-to-end: starting from a simulated detection alert (built on Week 11's cohort-baseline alerting concept), through Week 11's fault-vs-attack triage, into Week 12's dual-command containment/eradication/recovery runbook execution, and finishing with Week 12's evidence-packaging and reporting standards.

Evidence packaging for this deliverable means producing an organized evidence appendix (echoing the Week 8 midterm's format) showing your reasoning at each stage — what data source suggested compromise, how you distinguished it from a benign fault, and what evidence would need to be preserved in the described scenario.

Recovery narrative writing means producing the safety-reviewed restart sequence description (Week 9, Week 12) that a real operations team would need to follow, not just a statement that "the device was recovered."

7. Mathematical / Formal Foundations

No formula — instead, a completeness check against the drill's five stages above: a submission addressing only detection and containment, without evidence packaging and a full recovery narrative, has not completed the full lifecycle this deliverable is meant to exercise.

8. Practical Examples

Deliverable format: a written incident narrative walking through each of the five stages above for the capstone's provided scenario, with an evidence appendix (per Week 8's format) and a distinct, clearly labeled recovery-narrative section addressing the safety-sequencing concerns from Week 9's OT-aware ransomware-readiness lesson.

10. Security Perspective

This deliverable specifically tests whether you can execute the full IR lifecycle under the safety constraints this course has emphasized throughout, not just whether you can detect and technically remediate an issue in isolation from its physical-world context.

11. Industry Perspective

This mirrors a real post-incident report format used in mature security programs — reviewers specifically look for evidence of process discipline (each stage properly executed and documented), not just a correct final outcome.

12. Research Perspective

No new research content — the goal is integration of Weeks 9, 11, and 12's material under drill conditions, the same principle as the Week 8 midterm applied to the IR lifecycle specifically.

13. Case Study

Past cohorts' common deduction at this stage: skipping the recovery-narrative safety-sequencing section, treating "recovery" as a single step rather than the safety-reviewed sequence Week 9 and Week 12 specifically taught.

14. Common Misconceptions

  • "This drill is primarily about demonstrating technical detection skill." — It's equally about demonstrating process discipline across the full lifecycle, including safety-aware decision-making.
  • "Recovery just means confirming the device works again." — It requires a documented, safety-reviewed restart sequence narrative per Weeks 9 and 12's material.
  • "Evidence packaging is optional if the narrative is clear." — It's a required, separately graded component establishing the reasoning behind each stage's conclusions.
